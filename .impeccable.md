## Design Context

### Users
Two equal audiences who use the app together: **students aged 14–18** who initiate carpools and link their parents, and **parents aged 30–50** who join as drivers. Both open the app in a planning context — typically evening or morning before school — to organise group carpools to a fixed destination (school). Neither audience uses it under extreme time pressure; it's a setup and coordination tool, not a real-time dispatch tool.

### Brand Personality
**Reliable. Useful. Simple.**

SchoolLoop is a neighbourhood utility, not a consumer product. It should feel like the kind of tool you trust without thinking about — like a transit app or a school calendar. No personality quirks, no delight-for-delight's-sake. It earns trust by getting out of the way.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Reference**: Uber — confident typographic hierarchy, high contrast, utility-first layouts, nothing decorative that doesn't serve a function, equally considered light and dark modes.

**Anti-references**: Anything gamified (badges, streaks, confetti), anything that looks like a fintech dashboard (hero metrics, sparklines, gradient accents), anything social-app-ish (reactions, activity feeds, like counts).

**Theme**: Full light/dark system. Both modes equally designed — not one adapted from the other. Light mode for daytime planning, dark mode for evening/early morning.

**Visual tone**: Minimalist but not sterile. High contrast. Typography carries hierarchy rather than color or decoration. Sparse use of color — reserved for status and primary actions only.

### Design Principles
1. **Utility over expression** — Every element must solve a user problem. If it's decoration, remove it.
2. **Typography does the work** — Size and weight contrast create hierarchy. Color is for status only.
3. **Both modes, equally considered** — Light and dark are peers. Neither is an afterthought.
4. **Sparse color** — One primary action color (blue), semantic status colors (red/amber/green), nothing else. No gradients.
5. **No friction** — The most common action on each screen should be immediately obvious and reachable with one tap.
